# Americans With Disabilities

This section covers the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) requirements for disability nondiscrimination in employment (Title I) and access to financial institutions' goods, services, and facilities (Title III). Topics include essential job functions, reasonable accommodations, undue hardship, equal customer service, and readily achievable barrier removal. It also covers the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), technical standards for making websites accessible, including the perceivable and operable principles.

### Reference sources

- [EEOC: The ADA — Your Responsibilities as an Employer](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/ada-your-responsibilities-employer) — Employment protections, qualified individuals, essential functions, and reasonable accommodations.
- [U.S. Department of Justice: ADA Title III Regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations/) — Equal access to goods and services, reasonable modifications, and removal of barriers in existing facilities.
- [W3C: Accessibility Principles](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-principles/) — The four WCAG principles: perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust.

## Question 1

The WCAG are based on four principles, one of which is that a website must be operable. That means, among other things, that it can be navigated via:

- A. Voice
- B. A mouse
- C. A keyboard

**Correct answer: C. A keyboard.**

**Explanation:** WCAG's operable principle includes keyboard accessibility, so users can navigate and interact without relying on a mouse.

**Source:** [W3C: Understanding Success Criterion 2.1.1 — Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html).

## Question 2

Financial institutions are expected to do _____ to enable people with disabilities to access and enjoy _____ and benefits that are offered to people without a disability.

- A. Whatever is necessary; the most basic services
- B. Everything required by law; the most popular services
- C. Everything they reasonably can; all the services

**Correct answer: C. Everything they reasonably can; all the services.**

**Explanation:** The ADA requires equal access to the services offered by covered businesses, including banks, and reasonable modifications when needed. Access is not limited to basic or popular services.

**Source:** [U.S. Department of Justice: ADA Title III Regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations/).

## Question 3

The WCAG are based on four principles, one of which is that a website must be perceivable. That means it must be easy to:

- A. See and hear, with the capability to change nontext content into text
- B. Find on the internet and accessible with any browser
- C. Operate, so that users can avoid and correct mistakes

**Correct answer: A. See and hear, with the capability to change nontext content into text.**

**Explanation:** Perceivable means presenting information in ways users can perceive. This includes making content easier to see and hear and providing text alternatives for nontext content, such as descriptions of meaningful images.

**Source:** [W3C: Accessibility Principles](https://www.w3.org/WAI/fundamentals/accessibility-principles/).

## Question 4

The ADA does not require an employer to provide an accommodation if doing so would:

- A. Violate the employer's religious beliefs
- B. Attract people with disabilities
- C. Cause an undue hardship

**Correct answer: C. Cause an undue hardship.**

**Explanation:** Undue hardship means significant difficulty or expense, assessed in relation to the employer's resources and business operations.

**Source:** [EEOC: Small Employers and Reasonable Accommodation](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation).

## Question 5

Financial institutions must remove barriers in existing facilities when it is deemed:

- A. Absolutely necessary
- B. Within certain limits
- C. Readily achievable

**Correct answer: C. Readily achievable.**

**Explanation:** Readily achievable means barrier removal is easy to accomplish without much difficulty or expense, considering the business's resources and circumstances.

**Source:** [U.S. Department of Justice: ADA Title III Regulations, Section 36.304](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-iii-regulations/#section36304).

## Question 6

Employers are required to maintain fairness throughout the employment relationship. That includes:

- A. Only hiring and promotion or dismissal
- B. Only recruitment, testing, and hiring
- C. Recruitment, interviewing, testing, hiring, promotion, and dismissal

**Correct answer: C. Recruitment, interviewing, testing, hiring, promotion, and dismissal.**

**Explanation:** The ADA prohibits disability discrimination throughout the employment relationship, including application and selection procedures, advancement, and termination.

**Source:** [EEOC: The ADA — Questions and Answers](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/ada-questions-and-answers).

## Question 7

The ADA requires that people with disabilities have access to those goods and services that would otherwise be:

- A. Out of reach or unattainable to them
- B. Unthinkable or too expensive to them
- C. Out of sight or inappropriate to them

**Correct answer: A. Out of reach or unattainable to them.**

**Explanation:** The ADA requires covered businesses to provide equal access to their goods and services by addressing disability-related barriers.

**Source:** [U.S. Department of Justice: Businesses That Are Open to the Public](https://www.ada.gov/topics/title-iii/).

## Question 8

The ADA says that when interacting with people with disabilities, you should:

- A. Provide the same level of service that you would everyone else
- B. Execute their transactions for them
- C. Treat them exactly the way you would treat everyone else

**Correct answer: A. Provide the same level of service that you would everyone else.**

**Explanation:** Equal access may require reasonable modifications or communication assistance tailored to a person's disability. Identical treatment does not always provide equal access, and you should not assume someone needs you to complete their transaction for them.

**Source:** [U.S. Department of Justice: ADA Update — A Primer for Small Business](https://www.ada.gov/resources/title-iii-primer/).

## Question 9

Which of the following is an example of making reasonable accommodations for an employee with a disability?

- A. Making a position part time or establishing flexible hours
- B. Painting the walls in pastel hues
- C. Installing a TV on a wall mount in their office or cubicle so they can watch TV during breaks

**Correct answer: A. Making a position part time or establishing flexible hours.**

**Explanation:** Part-time or modified work schedules can be reasonable accommodations when needed because of a disability, provided they do not cause undue hardship.

**Source:** [EEOC: Small Employers and Reasonable Accommodation](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation).

## Question 10

The ADA says employers cannot, on the basis of a disability or perceived disability, discriminate against a qualified individual, defined as someone who fulfills all the job-related requirements, with or without reasonable accommodation, and can perform _____ of that position.

- A. The minimum duties
- B. The essential function
- C. Anything that might arise in carrying out

**Correct answer: B. The essential function.**

**Explanation:** The ADA uses the term "essential functions": the fundamental duties of the position. A qualified individual meets the job's required qualifications and can perform those essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation.

**Source:** [EEOC: The ADA — Your Responsibilities as an Employer](https://www.eeoc.gov/publications/ada-your-responsibilities-employer).

# Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices

## Question 1

What is the term for the illegal practice of assessing multiple late fees when there was one late payment and the payment, but not the late fee, was subsequently made?

- A. Stacking
- B. Pyramiding
- C. Loading

**Correct answer: B. Pyramiding.**

**Explanation:** Pyramiding occurs when additional late fees are charged solely because an earlier late fee remains unpaid, even though subsequent required payments are made in full and on time.

**Source:** [CFPB: Regulation Z, Section 1026.36(c)(2) — No Pyramiding of Late Fees](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/36/#c-2).

## Question 2

A material claim is one that:

- A. Conveys two or more meanings to a reasonable consumer
- B. Causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to a consumer
- C. Involves important information that is likely to affect a consumer's decision to purchase or use a product or service

**Correct answer: C. Involves important information that is likely to affect a consumer's decision to purchase or use a product or service.**

**Explanation:** Materiality concerns whether information is likely to influence a consumer's choice or conduct regarding a product or service. Substantial consumer injury is part of the separate unfairness analysis.

**Source:** [FTC: Advertising FAQs — A Guide for Small Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business).

## Question 3

Regulators may be more likely to focus on a UDAAP complaint if they feel the incident triggering it is:

- A. Unlike anything that's happened before
- B. Worthy of the public's attention
- C. Part of a pattern or practice

**Correct answer: C. Part of a pattern or practice.**

**Explanation:** Complaint patterns can indicate recurring misconduct affecting multiple consumers and warrant closer regulatory review. A single complaint can also justify investigation; a pattern is not required for a violation.

**Source:** [OCC: Comptroller's Handbook — Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices and Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/unfair-deceptive-act/pub-ch-udap-udaap.pdf).

## Question 4

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau is not the only agency charged with enforcing the current UDAAP standards. What is one of the others?

- A. The Treasury Department
- B. The Department of Justice
- C. The Federal Trade Commission

**Best answer: C. The Federal Trade Commission.**

**Explanation:** The FTC enforces prohibitions on unfair or deceptive acts or practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The question uses UDAAP loosely: the FTC's Section 5 authority covers UDAP (unfair or deceptive), rather than the separate Dodd-Frank prohibition on abusive acts or practices.

**Source:** [FTC: Enforcement Authority](https://www.ftc.gov/about-ftc/mission/enforcement-authority).

## Question 5

Which of the following actions could lead to a UDAAP issue?

- A. Advertising a new low interest rate on home loans
- B. Saying the final payment on a loan will be a balloon payment
- C. Calling home equity free money

**Correct answer: C. Calling home equity free money.**

**Explanation:** In a lending advertisement, calling home equity "free money" could mislead consumers about repayment obligations, interest, and the risk of losing their home. Advertising an accurate rate or disclosing a balloon payment is not inherently deceptive.

**Source:** [CFPB: What Is a Home Equity Loan?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-loan-en-106/); [FTC: Advertising FAQs — A Guide for Small Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business).

## Question 6

Which of the following is an example of a practice that cannot be reasonably avoided by consumers and is therefore unfair?

- A. The requesting of credit information from a consumer credit agency when the consumer applies for a loan
- B. The charging of a fee for non-account holders' use of an institution's ATM
- C. The withholding of price information until after the consumer has committed to buying

**Correct answer: C. The withholding of price information until after the consumer has committed to buying.**

**Explanation:** Withholding material price information prevents consumers from making an informed decision before committing. The full unfairness test also requires substantial actual or likely consumer injury that is not outweighed by benefits to consumers or competition; lack of reasonable avoidance alone is not sufficient.

**Source:** [FDIC: Guidance on Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2004/fil2604a.html).

## Question 7

When dealing with third-party service providers, you should pay special attention to _____ that would create _____ to engage in unfair or deceptive practices.

- A. Compensation agreements; unintended incentives
- B. Unreasonable deadlines; undue pressures
- C. Ambiguous language; unforeseen opportunities

**Correct answer: A. Compensation agreements; unintended incentives.**

**Explanation:** Payment arrangements can unintentionally reward harmful conduct, such as steering consumers toward higher-cost products. Institutions should review third-party compensation to ensure it does not encourage unfair or deceptive practices.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — Third Party Risk](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-4-third-party-risk).

## Question 8

A deceptive act or practice _____ be cured by subsequent truthful statements.

- A. Cannot
- B. Can occasionally
- C. Can

**Correct answer: A. Cannot.**

**Explanation:** Later truthful disclosures do not erase an earlier deceptive act or practice. Institutions must ensure their representations are not misleading from the outset.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 9

A three-part test is used to determine whether a representation, omission, or practice is deceptive. One of the parts asks whether a consumer's:

- A. Interpretation is reasonable under the circumstances
- B. Disaffection is based on fact
- C. Professed injury is substantive, emotional, or simply trivial

**Correct answer: A. Interpretation is reasonable under the circumstances.**

**Explanation:** The deception test asks whether the representation, omission, or practice misleads or is likely to mislead; whether the consumer's interpretation is reasonable under the circumstances; and whether the misleading information is material to the consumer's decision.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 10

For what kind of payment does Regulation Z require that you clearly state that there is such a payment and that you disclose the amount and timing of that payment if the consumer only makes minimum payments?

- A. Parachute
- B. Balloon
- C. Bucket

**Correct answer: B. Balloon.**

**Explanation:** A balloon payment requires repayment of the remaining balance in a lump sum. For home equity plan advertisements stating a minimum periodic payment, Regulation Z requires disclosure when a balloon payment results, including its amount and timing if the consumer makes minimum payments for the maximum permitted period.

**Source:** [Federal Reserve: Regulation Z, Section 1026.16 — Advertising](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-102616-advertising.htm).

## Question 11

Acts or practices that have the potential to mislead include:

- A. Announced price increase
- B. Free checking that comes with no monthly fees
- C. Bait-and-switch techniques

**Correct answer: C. Bait-and-switch techniques.**

**Explanation:** Bait-and-switch techniques lure consumers with an advertised offer and then steer them toward a different, often more expensive product instead of honoring the offer. The FDIC identifies these techniques as potentially deceptive.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 12

Withholding price information until after the consumer has committed to buy is an example of _____, this condition being one of three that would make a practice unfair.

- A. Sloppiness on the part of the people who proofread ads
- B. Eliminating information that could confuse the consumer
- C. A consumer not being able to reasonably avoid injury

**Correct answer: C. A consumer not being able to reasonably avoid injury.**

**Explanation:** Consumers need material price information before committing so they can make an informed choice and avoid harm. The three unfairness criteria are substantial actual or likely injury, injury that consumers cannot reasonably avoid, and injury not outweighed by benefits to consumers or competition.

**Source:** [FDIC: Guidance on Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2004/fil2604a.html).

## Question 13

An act or practice that materially interferes with a consumer's ability to understand a term or condition is defined as:

- A. Abuse
- B. Deceptive
- C. Unfair

**Correct answer: A. Abuse.**

**Explanation:** The legal term is an "abusive" act or practice. Materially interfering with a consumer's ability to understand a term or condition of a consumer financial product or service is one basis for finding abusiveness under the Dodd-Frank Act.

**Source:** [12 U.S.C. Section 5531(d): Abusive Acts or Practices](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A12+section%3A5531+edition%3Aprelim%29).

## Question 14

An act or practice is unfair if it causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers. In this context, substantial injury usually means _____ harm.

- A. Monetary
- B. Trivial or speculative
- C. Emotional

**Correct answer: A. Monetary.**

**Explanation:** Substantial injury usually involves financial harm. Even small losses affecting many consumers can qualify. Nonmonetary harm can qualify in some circumstances, but trivial or merely speculative harm generally does not. Substantial injury is one of three required elements of the unfairness test.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 15

When a complaint is recognized, it's important for financial institutions to:

- A. Uncover the root cause of the problem
- B. Engage in damage control
- C. Ensure the guilty employees are punished

**Correct answer: A. Uncover the root cause of the problem.**

**Explanation:** Identifying the underlying cause helps the institution correct the problem, address consumer harm, and prevent recurrence.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — Communicating Findings](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/ii-6-communicating-findings).

## Question 16

It's vitally important that your financial institution has in place an effective system for _____ complaints.

- A. Tracking and resolving
- B. Monitoring and diverting
- C. Fielding and rebutting

**Correct answer: A. Tracking and resolving.**

**Explanation:** Institutions should record complaints, investigate and resolve the issues raised, and monitor trends that may indicate recurring compliance problems.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 17

Which of the following is a consumer claim or allegation that might indicate UDAAP?

- A. Uncompetitive interest rates
- B. Unsolicited credit card offers
- C. Inability to reach a service representative

**Correct answer: C. Inability to reach a service representative.**

**Explanation:** The FDIC identifies complaints about inability to reach customer service as a possible warning sign of UDAP or UDAAP. Such a complaint warrants review but does not, by itself, establish a violation.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 18

Which of the following statutes is among those concerned with UDAAP?

- A. Community Reinvestment Act
- B. Equal Credit Opportunity Act
- C. Bank Secrecy Act

**Best answer: B. Equal Credit Opportunity Act.**

**Explanation:** ECOA prohibits discrimination in credit transactions and is a related consumer protection statute considered alongside UDAP/UDAAP requirements. It does not itself establish the general UDAAP prohibition; that comes from Dodd-Frank, with the FTC Act separately prohibiting unfair or deceptive practices.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank); [FDIC: Fair Lending Laws and Regulations](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/iv-1-fair-lending-laws-and-regulations).

## Question 19

What is one of the reasons it's important for frontline staff to report complaints to their superiors?

- A. To preserve the good reputation of the institution
- B. To protect themselves from the possibility of physical harm from a disgruntled consumer
- C. To meet any reporting quotas

**Correct answer: A. To preserve the good reputation of the institution.**

**Explanation:** Escalating complaints allows management to address customer concerns and recurring problems promptly, helping maintain trust and protect the institution's reputation.

**Source:** [OCC: Comptroller's Handbook — Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices and Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/unfair-deceptive-act/pub-ch-udap-udaap.pdf).

## Question 20

For an act or practice to be unfair, the injury also must not be outweighed by any _____ produced by the act or practice.

- A. Unforeseen consequences to the benefit of competitors
- B. Unexpected profits of the institution
- C. Offsetting consumer or competitive benefits

**Correct answer: C. Offsetting consumer or competitive benefits.**

**Explanation:** The unfairness test requires that consumer injury not be outweighed by countervailing benefits to consumers or competition. This is assessed along with substantial injury and consumers' inability to reasonably avoid that injury.

**Source:** [FDIC: Consumer Compliance Examination Manual — UDAP and UDAAP](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual/vii-1-federal-trade-commission-act-section-5-and-dodd-frank).

## Question 21

Which of the following incidents was both unfair and abusive rather than deceptive?

- A. An institution advertised free checking accounts but did not properly disclose the minimum account activity to keep the account free
- B. Institution employees opened accounts without consumers' knowledge by making unauthorized transfers from consumers' authorized accounts
- C. An institution's marketing of a service led credit card holders to believe they were updating their accounts or agreeing to receive information about the service when, in fact, they were purchasing it

**Correct answer: B. Institution employees opened accounts without consumers' knowledge by making unauthorized transfers from consumers' authorized accounts.**

**Explanation:** This matches the unauthorized-account practices the CFPB found unfair and abusive in its 2016 Wells Fargo action. The conduct caused financial harm consumers could not reasonably avoid and took unreasonable advantage of their inability to protect their interests. Options A and C describe misleading disclosures or marketing. These legal categories can overlap; "unfair and abusive" does not mean conduct could never also be deceptive.

**Source:** [CFPB: Wells Fargo Enforcement Action — Director's Remarks](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/prepared-remarks-richard-cordray-director-consumer-financial-protection-bureau-wells-fargo-enforcement-action-press-call/).

# Bank Secrecy

## Question 1

You must write down how you identified the person when filling out a _____.

- A. SAR
- B. CTR
- C. Credit report

**Correct answer: B. CTR.**

**Explanation:** A Currency Transaction Report (CTR) requires documentation of the method used to verify the person's identity, such as a driver's license and its identifying details.

**Source:** [FinCEN: Currency Transaction Report Electronic Filing Requirements](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/docs/XMLUserGuide_FinCENCTR.pdf).

## Question 2

File a SAR with FinCEN if a suspected money laundering transaction or series of transactions involves _____ or more in funds or other assets.

- A. $5,000
- B. $1,000
- C. $2,500

**Correct answer: A. $5,000.**

**Explanation:** For banks, transactions conducted or attempted that involve or aggregate at least $5,000 and meet the suspected money laundering criteria require a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR). This threshold applies to this reporting category; other SAR requirements can have different thresholds.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Suspicious Activity Reporting](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/04).

## Question 3

When confirming the identity of a customer under your CIP procedures, you should receive proper identification _____ opening an account.

- A. Within 5 days of
- B. Before
- C. After

**Best answer: B. Before.**

**Explanation:** Required identifying information generally must be collected before opening an account. The question simplifies the rule: verification of identity may be completed within a reasonable time after opening under the bank's risk-based Customer Identification Program (CIP). There is no universal five-day deadline.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Customer Identification Program — Examination Procedures](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/01_ep).

## Question 4

Several pieces of information are needed to file a CTR about a person who conducts a transaction, as well as any person on whose behalf the transaction is being conducted. That information does not include a person's:

- A. Name
- B. Date of birth
- C. Credit history

**Correct answer: C. Credit history.**

**Explanation:** A CTR collects identifying information about individuals involved in the transaction, including name and date of birth. Credit history is not required CTR information.

**Source:** [FinCEN: Currency Transaction Report Electronic Filing Requirements](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/docs/XMLUserGuide_FinCENCTR.pdf).

## Question 5

The BSA requires you to record purchases of checks, cashier's checks, money orders, and traveler's checks made with _____ in currency.

- A. $2,500 to $7,500
- B. $1,500 to $8,000
- C. $3,000 to $10,000

**Correct answer: C. $3,000 to $10,000.**

**Explanation:** The monetary instrument recordkeeping requirement applies to currency purchases of covered instruments from $3,000 through $10,000, inclusive. Covered instruments include bank checks and drafts, cashier's checks, money orders, and traveler's checks.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Purchase and Sale of Certain Monetary Instruments Recordkeeping](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/08).

## Question 6

The BSA requires depository institutions to keep records of funds transfers and transmittals of _____ or more.

- A. $1,000
- B. $3,000
- C. $2,500

**Correct answer: B. $3,000.**

**Explanation:** Banks must collect and retain specified information for covered funds transfers of $3,000 or more. Certain transfers are excluded or exempt from this requirement.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Funds Transfers Recordkeeping](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/09).

## Question 7

A _____ may be treated as a nonlisted business if it otherwise meets the requirements for such a business.

- A. Limited liability company
- B. Sole proprietorship
- C. Cooperative

**Correct answer: B. Sole proprietorship.**

**Explanation:** The CTR exemption rule expressly permits a sole proprietorship to be treated as a nonlisted business if it meets the applicable eligibility requirements. This is not an automatic exemption.

**Source:** [FinCEN: Chapter X Final Rule, 31 CFR 1020.315(e)(7)](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/ChaptXFinalRule.pdf).

## Question 8

Some institutions consider cédula cards in Latin American countries, or _____ ID cards issued by the Mexican government, to be acceptable forms of identification.

- A. Consular
- B. Personal
- C. Voter

**Correct answer: A. Consular.**

**Explanation:** The Mexican consular identification card is known as the matrícula consular. Acceptance depends on the institution's Customer Identification Program procedures.

**Source:** [U.S. Department of State: Mexico — Civil Documents](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country/Mexico.html); [FFIEC: Customer Identification Program](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/01).

## Question 9

There are two levels of beneficial ownership, both of which must be determined for most legal entities. They are:

- A. Ownership and control
- B. Leadership and administration
- C. Legal and financial

**Correct answer: A. Ownership and control.**

**Explanation:** FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence rule uses two "prongs": ownership (each individual, if any, who directly or indirectly owns 25% or more of the entity's equity) and control (one individual with significant responsibility to control, manage, or direct the entity). The same person may satisfy both prongs, and exclusions or exemptions apply to certain entities.

**Source:** [FinCEN: CDD Rule FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-and-regulations/cdd-rule-faqs).

## Question 10

CTRs must be filed electronically and must be submitted by the _____ after the day of the transaction or transactions.

- A. 10th day
- B. 15th day
- C. 3rd day

**Correct answer: B. 15th day.**

**Explanation:** A CTR must be electronically filed with FinCEN by the 15th calendar day after the day of the reportable transaction or transactions. The deadline is measured in calendar days, not business days.

**Source:** [FinCEN: Frequently Asked Questions Regarding the Currency Transaction Report](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/frequently-asked-questions-regarding-fincen-currency-transaction-report-ctr).

## Question 11

Financial institutions must give a _____ report to the IRS on persons who own an interest in or who have authority over a financial account in a foreign country that has, at any point during the calendar year, an aggregate value exceeding $10,000.

- A. Yearly
- B. Monthly
- C. Biannual

**Best answer: A. Yearly.**

**Explanation:** The Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) is an annual report.

**Wording correction:** The question misstates the filing obligation and recipient. Generally, the U.S. person with a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign financial accounts must file an FBAR with FinCEN when their aggregate value exceeds $10,000 at any time during the calendar year, subject to applicable exceptions. This is not a general requirement for financial institutions to report their customers to the IRS. The IRS has FBAR enforcement responsibilities, but the report is filed electronically with FinCEN.

**Source:** [IRS: Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR)](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar).

## Question 12

A DOEP form has to be filed within _____ days after the day of the first reportable currency transaction that is supposed to be exempt.

- A. 15
- B. 10
- C. 30

**Correct answer: C. 30.**

**Explanation:** When a Designation of Exempt Person (DOEP) filing is required, the bank must file it within 30 days after the first reportable currency transaction it wishes to exempt from CTR reporting.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Transactions of Exempt Persons — Examination Procedures](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/06_ep).

## Question 13

If the institution involved in a wire transfer is the originator's institution, records must be referenced to the name of the:

## Question 15

File both a CTR and SAR if a transaction you suspect to be money laundering is in excess of _____ in currency.

- A. $1,500
- B. $7,500
- C. $10,000

**Correct answer: C. $10,000.**

**Explanation:** A nonexempt currency transaction exceeding $10,000 requires a CTR. If it also meets the bank's SAR criteria for suspected money laundering, a SAR is required as well; neither report replaces the other. The CTR threshold is more than $10,000, not exactly $10,000.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Currency Transaction Reporting](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/05); [FFIEC: Suspicious Activity Reporting](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/04).

You have to keep CTRs on file for _____ years.

## Question 17

For a person with nonexempt accounts, any currency transaction or series of currency transactions in a single day totaling more than _____, of which the financial institution has knowledge, must be reported to the federal government on a CTR.

- A. $5,000
- B. $2,500
- C. $10,000

**Correct answer: C. $10,000.**

**Explanation:** Known currency transactions by or on behalf of the same person must be aggregated during one business day. A CTR is required when cash in or cash out exceeds $10,000. Cash in and cash out are calculated separately, not netted against each other.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Currency Transaction Reporting](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/05).

## Question 18

One type of exemption from reporting requirements is an _____ exemption.

- A. Automatic
- B. Assumed
- C. Applied

**Best answer: A. Automatic.**

**Explanation:** "Automatic" is training shorthand for certain CTR exemptions that do not require a DOEP filing, such as eligible banks' domestic operations and qualifying government entities. Institutions must still establish eligibility; CTR exemptions do not remove SAR obligations. Official guidance groups exempt persons into Phase I and Phase II categories.

**Source:** [FFIEC: Transactions of Exempt Persons](https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual/AssessingComplianceWithBSARegulatoryRequirements/06).

## Question 19

If a trust owns 25 percent or more of the equity in a legal entity, then beneficial owner includes the:

**Correct answer: B. Trustee.**

## Question 20

Which form of identification would not be sufficient to positively identify a customer if used alone?

- A. U.S. passport
- B. Employer ID card
- C. A valid driver's license with a photograph

**Correct answer: B. Employer ID card.**

**Explanation:** An ordinary employer ID card alone generally does not provide the assurance of identity expected under CIP procedures. Standard documentary verification uses unexpired government-issued identification evidencing nationality or residence and bearing a photograph or similar safeguard, such as a passport or driver's license. Acceptable verification methods depend on the institution's CIP.

**Transcription note:** Option A was supplied as "US password" and is interpreted as "U.S. passport."
